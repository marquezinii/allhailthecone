# An orientation to the impossible

## Before the first minute

The earliest surviving record is a seating plan.

It shows an orange cone, a circle of small chairs, and a handwritten request for more tea. The section marked “reason for assembly” is blank. There is no evidence that anyone considered this an omission.

Around that gathering, a civilization grew. Or perhaps a civilization finally noticed where it had been gathering all along. The Office of Continuity has retained both descriptions pending better handwriting.

## The Stillwater Concord

Across the floating archipelago, separate islands agree on a few useful things: travellers should be welcomed, routes should be maintained, and nobody should be left without bread because their home has drifted outside the approved delivery radius.

This agreement is the **Stillwater Concord**. It is a compact among communities, not an empire of conquest. Aurelian Stillwater wears the crown, chairs its council and goes to see the places about which difficult decisions are being made.

There is considerable ceremony. There is also a kettle within reach of every meeting.

## A centre that does not command

The Sacred Cone occupies the centre of Stillwater Seat. It has never spoken, issued a law, asked for a crown or explained why its presence matters.

The Little Order keeps its surroundings clean. Travellers leave lights beside it. Officials make an effort to walk past before important departures. Some believe the Cone offers direction. Others believe it makes a useful place to meet. These people often share a bench.

No approved doctrine can demonstrate that the Cone causes miracles. Nor does the archive claim to have proved that it does not.

## The business of staying together

Percival Crumb's Bread Operations maps supply routes that shift with the islands. K. Musa maintains the machinery that makes those routes survivable. Octavia Resonant gives departures a rhythm and arrivals a welcome.

Their departments disagree. A beacon repair may close the only route to a bakery. A festival may need the bridge that a surveyor has declared temporarily metaphorical. These are serious problems for the people involved.

The work of the Concord is not to eliminate strangeness. It is to make a life within it.

## The cup before the crossing

Tea is both a drink and a civic pause. To offer a cup is to promise a moment in which nobody has to justify being tired, lost or afraid. Aurelian uses this custom in diplomacy, but it belongs to everyone.

The hospitality compact does not prohibit urgent action. It prohibits manufacturing urgency to avoid listening.

## A report from the outer route

_Semi-Canon field fragment. Author unrecorded._

> The new island arrived three days behind its own mail. We found six houses, a closed bakery and a bell that rang only when someone listened.
>
> His Majesty asked whether the residents had eaten.
>
> The survey will resume after breakfast.

## The horizon stays open

The Unfiled Expanse begins beyond the last maintained beacon. Shapes larger than cities pass through distant portals. A few islands have received letters bearing their own seals in an unfamiliar hand.

Exploration continues. So do meals, repairs and small kindnesses. Whatever waits beyond the next crossing will encounter a civilization that has already made space for another chair.
